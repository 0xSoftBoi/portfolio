Research standard · v1.0

Make every pass harder to fool.

This is the publication loop I use for technical research notes: primary sources before prose, claims before rhetoric, explicit evidence classes, quantitative validation where possible, a serious rival explanation, and a revision path after publication.

Acceptance gates

Five gates before a piece earns “research.”

A polished argument can still be wrong. These gates are designed to stop presentation quality from laundering weak evidence.

Gate A · Provenance

Can every consequential claim be traced?

Prefer primary documents, source code, filings, papers, raw measurements, protocol specifications, or direct experiment artifacts. Secondary sources are context, not the foundation.

Gate B · Quantitative validity

Does the number survive basic attacks?

State denominator, units, comparison set, measurement window, assumptions, and uncertainty. Run sensitivity or alternative specifications when the conclusion depends on a parameter choice.

Gate C · Adversarial thesis

What is the strongest explanation that makes me wrong?

Steelman the rival mechanism and test it. If it explains the evidence as well as the preferred thesis, narrow the conclusion instead of writing around the ambiguity.

Gate D · Citation integrity

Does the source support the exact sentence?

No citation laundering. A citation must support the specific factual or quantitative claim adjacent to it. Distinguish facts from derivations, estimates, forecasts, and engineering targets.

Gate E · Reproducibility

Can another person inspect or recreate the result?

Link code, data, queries, configs, calculation notebooks, benchmark prompts, or hardware logs where practical. If reproduction is impossible, explain exactly why.

Evidence classes

Label what kind of truth a sentence claims.

The article should never make an estimate look observed or an engineering target look achieved.

ObservedMeasured directly in an experiment, system, filing, trace, or dataset.
DerivedCalculated from observed inputs with an explicit method.
EstimatedModel-dependent or inferred; assumptions must be exposed.
TargetA design requirement, future gate, or intended performance level.
Article anatomy

The minimum research package.

  1. Decision question. Why does the reader care whether the thesis is true?
  2. One-sentence claim. A falsifiable statement, not a topic.
  3. Key findings. Three to five findings with numbers where the evidence permits them.
  4. Method. Inputs, measurement procedure, comparison group, assumptions, and exclusions.
  5. Evidence ledger. Hard claims mapped to primary sources and evidence classes.
  6. Results. Tables/figures that can be understood without ornamental storytelling.
  7. Counterargument. The strongest alternative mechanism and what the data says about it.
  8. Limitations. What the work does not establish.
  9. Falsification/update condition. What future observation should change the conclusion.
  10. Reproducibility + revision log. Artifacts and dated corrections.
