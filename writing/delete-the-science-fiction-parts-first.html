Delete the science-fiction parts first

The first useful prototype of an airborne drone carrier is not a smaller carrier. It is the cheapest machine that can falsify the riskiest interaction in the architecture.

Claim. Ambitious hardware becomes tractable when the prototype is designed around the architecture's highest-risk interaction rather than around visual resemblance to the final system. For Aiur, that means deleting almost everything until the program asks one question: can a buoyant carrier repeatedly recover a small autonomous aircraft onto a moving dock?

The original picture is a trap

The motivating idea is deliberately excessive: a persistent lighter-than-air carrier deploying and recovering a fleet of autonomous aircraft, eventually carrying coordination compute and energy infrastructure aloft.

If the first prototype tries to look like that picture, it inherits every hard problem at once: a large envelope, lift-gas tradeoffs, outdoor operation, multiple aircraft, perception, charging, high-power compute, thermal management, communications, and docking.

A prototype with that much surface area can fail without telling you which assumption killed it.

A prototype should maximize information per failure, not resemblance to the product rendering.

P0 removes almost everything

CARRIER-P0 uses helium, one active belly dock, one small aircraft first, externally referenced indoor positioning, tethered/guarded operation, and no airborne charging. The baseline reference article is a roughly 4.5 m indoor airship; the aircraft is a Crazyflie-class micro-UAV.

Hydrogen is excluded. Outdoor/BVLOS operation is excluded. Eight heavy aircraft are excluded. A 40 m envelope is excluded. DGX-class airborne compute is excluded. Swarm behavior arrives only after one-aircraft recovery is repeatable.

Those are not compromises to make the concept less interesting. They are deletions that preserve the one interaction capable of invalidating the whole architecture: recovery.

The dock is a state machine, not a magnet

The current design uses a wide capture funnel and lightweight probe, then requires two independent physical signals before the aircraft may disarm: a seat switch confirms the probe reached the seat, and a keeper switch confirms a positive mechanical lock closed underneath it.

The intended rule is S1 AND S2, not “the vehicle seems attached.” An electromagnet is not required to maintain attachment. That turns docking from a perception event into a mechanically checkable state transition.

The acceptance ladder prevents premature scale

The program is staged so each gate removes a different class of uncertainty.

P0-A: bench capture—mass, repeated mechanical cycles, independent sensor confirmation, screening loads, emergency release.
P0-B: suspended moving dock—autonomous terminal approach and repeated capture without prop/funnel contact.
P0-C: tethered helium carrier—complete autonomous launch/recovery cycles with zero envelope strikes.
P0-D: only then introduce two-aircraft sequencing.

Airborne compute and charging are downstream gates. If recovery does not work, optimizing GPU power budgets is theater.

Targets are not results

The repository contains concrete targets: a 180 mm funnel entrance, commanded closing speed limits, mass ceilings, repeated capture goals, and zero-strike requirements. These numbers exist to make failure legible. They are not achieved performance.

That distinction is why this is a design note rather than a research report. The repo has executable mass/capture models, a fail-safe dock controller, fabrication packets, CAD, and evidence-reduction tooling. It does not yet have the physical recovery dataset required to claim the architecture works.

Engineering maturity is partly the ability to say which numbers are measurements and which are promises to yourself.

The strongest counterargument

Indoor micro-UAV docking may be so far from a large outdoor carrier that success provides little evidence about the final platform. That is a real risk. P0 is not a scale-law proof.

Its purpose is more modest: falsify the recovery architecture cheaply before solving scale. If a positive mechanical capture interface cannot achieve repeated controlled recovery under forgiving indoor conditions, there is little reason to add wind, larger aircraft, charging contacts, and outdoor navigation.

If P0 succeeds, it removes one uncertainty and leaves many others alive. That is what a prototype is supposed to do.

What would promote this from design note to research?

A committed dataset of instrumented attempts: approach error, closing speed, capture outcome, unsafe contacts, session conditions, and a script that regenerates the acceptance result. Until that exists, every performance number stays labeled target.
